The game

Nineteen levels.
One narrator who

You play a small cream-coloured block in a red scarf. Walk right, reach the door. Simple, until the floor lies, the lights go out, the walls turn out to be suggestions and a voice keeps commenting on how you're doing. Every trap is hand-placed and fair. It just doesn't feel that way.

How to play

Three steps.
Then a lot of dying.

1

Open it

Use a laptop or desktop with a keyboard. The first visit downloads the game once, so give it a moment. After that it opens fast.

2

Press Play

Pick Campaign for the 19 levels, or Race the Bot to beat the solver's time. Normal or Brutal: your call. Brutal keeps its own progress.

3

Reach the door

Walk right, find the exit portal, don't trust anything. Die, learn, go again. The checkpoint restart is one key away.

I'm a gamer first

I've loved games for as long as I can remember. I've spent more hours inside other people's worlds than I'd like to admit, and somewhere along the way I started wondering how any of it was actually made.

Then, in college, a project came along. We had to build something, and most people picked something safe. I picked a game.

It was really bad

I'll be honest: the first version was bad. The movement felt wrong, the levels were boring, and the whole thing held together mostly out of stubbornness. It did its job for the project. It was not something I was proud of, and I didn't show it to anyone I wanted to impress.

So I closed the folder and moved on.

Then it just sat there

For a few months, nothing. Other work took over. The game stayed exactly where I'd left it, half-finished, quietly judging me from a directory I never opened.

It wasn't a school assignment anymore. It was a game I wanted to exist.

One day I opened it again

I can't tell you what changed. Gaming is my passion, and one day I picked the project back up and asked a different question: what would this be if I built it like a real game, not like homework?

So I rebuilt it properly. I fixed the movement until it felt right. I wrote levels that lie to you on purpose and learned how to make a trap feel fair even when it kills you. I gave the game a narrator who is quietly disappointed in you, and a red-scarfed block you can't help rooting for.

It grew into nineteen levels in three acts, two difficulties, fourteen characters, a Spite currency, awards, and a bot you can race.

Behind the build

  • Written in Python with pygame-ce, and packaged for the browser with Pygbag.
  • There are no image or sound files. Every picture, sound effect and music track is generated by code.
  • The rules have no randomness, so every trap is the same every time.
  • A solver bot plays the game itself. It cleared all 19 levels on Normal and all 19 on Brutal, proving each one can be beaten.
  • Around 8,000 lines of code and an automated test suite keep it all honest.

And I'm not done

This is the first game I've actually finished, and it taught me more than any class could. I'm going to keep building games, bigger and better, and every one of them will start the same way: a gamer who can't stop wondering what he could make.

If you play this one, die a lot, and laugh at least once, it worked.

FAQ

Questions nobody asked

Is it really free?

Yes. No download, no account, no ads, no sign-up. Open the page and play.

What do I need to play?

A laptop or desktop with a recent browser and a keyboard. Touch controls and gamepad support exist, but a keyboard is the proper way to suffer.

Why does the first load take a moment?

The whole game, including its music and sound, is downloaded once. Next time it opens much faster.

Will my progress be saved?

The game keeps its save in your browser on your device, so there's no account. Clearing your site data for this page will reset it.

What's the difference between Normal and Brutal?

Brutal is the harder setting, and it keeps its own separate progress, so playing one never touches the other.

Is there anything I should be careful about?

The game has flashing lights and screen shake. You can turn both down in Settings. Headphones are recommended.

Will there be more games?

Yes. This is the first, not the last.
